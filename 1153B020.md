### 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】
**What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

**Answer:**
* **Peptide bond** (a covalent bond): Formed between the carboxyl group ($-\text{COOH}$) of one amino acid and the amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) of another through a condensation reaction, releasing a water molecule.
* **Backbone Interactions:** All standard amino acids can form hydrogen bonds with one another via their backbone carbonyl oxygen ($\text{C=O}$) and amide hydrogen ($\text{N-H}$) groups to construct secondary structures like $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-sheets.
* **Side-chain Interactions:** Amino acids with polar or charged side chains can form hydrogen bonds with each other:
  * **Hydroxyl groups:** Serine (Ser), Threonine (Thr), Tyrosine (Tyr)
  * **Amide groups:** Asparagine (Asn), Glutamine (Gln)
  * **Carboxyl groups:** Aspartate/Aspartic acid (Asp), Glutamate/Glutamic acid (Glu)
  * **Basic nitrogen groups:** Lysine (Lys), Arginine (Arg), Histidine (His)

### 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】
**How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

**Answer:**
Nucleotides join along a single strand via covalent phosphodiester bonds to form a continuous sugar-phosphate backbone. Two antiparallel strands (running in opposite $5' \to 3'$ and $3' \to 5'$ directions) align and bind together through hydrogen bonds formed between complementary nitrogenous bases in the center, spiraling into a double helix. 

The basic structural components of each nucleotide are a phosphate group, a deoxyribose sugar, and one of four nitrogenous bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), or Cytosine (C). Standard DNA (B-DNA) is **right-handed**. Specific hydrogen bonding occurs between complementary base pairs: Adenine pairs with Thymine through two hydrogen bonds ($\text{A}=\text{T}$) and Guanine pairs with Cytosine through three hydrogen bonds ($\text{G}\equiv\text{C}$).

### 3. 【Searching for My One-and-Only Soulmate in the Universe: A 20-Base-Pair Love Code Hidden Among 3 Billion DNA Base Pairs】
**The source of molecular stability and specificity: hydrogen bonding — How do TP53 and DNA recognize each other? Using the structure 3KMD, identify one pair of an amino acid and a DNA base that interact with each other.**

**Answer:**
The tumor suppressor protein TP53 achieves sequence-specific recognition of its target DNA binding sites primarily through hydrogen bonding networks established between its core DNA-binding domain (DBD) residues and specific functional groups within the major groove of the DNA double helix. In the crystal structure of the TP53–DNA complex (PDB: **3KMD**), this molecular specificity is exemplified by the direct interaction between **Arginine 280 (Arg280)** of the TP53 protein and **Guanine (G)** of the DNA target, where the positively charged guanidinium group of the Arg280 side chain forms base-specific hydrogen bonds with the oxygen and nitrogen acceptors on the guanine base.

### 4. 【Return to the Crime Scene】
**Why can a mutation (change) in just one amino acid of a protein sometimes lead to a serious human disease? Give one specific example of such a disease.**

**Answer:**
A mutation in just one amino acid—known as a single amino acid substitution or missense mutation—can cause serious human disease because a protein's function is strictly dependent on its three-dimensional structure and chemical properties. Amino acids differ in size, charge, hydrophobicity, and hydrogen-bonding ability. Replacing a single amino acid can disrupt critical secondary or tertiary folding, destabilize the protein core, alter an active/binding site, or interfere with molecular interactions, ultimately leading to a loss of protein function or toxic misfolding.

A classic example of such a disease is **Sickle Cell Anemia**. It is caused by a single point mutation in the *HBB* gene, which changes the sixth amino acid in the $\beta$-globin chain of hemoglobin from a hydrophilic **glutamic acid (Glu)** to a hydrophobic **valine (Val)**. This single substitution creates a hydrophobic patch that causes hemoglobin molecules to stick together and polymerize into rigid fibers under low oxygen conditions, distorting red blood cells into a sickle shape that blocks blood vessels and causes severe pain and anemia.
